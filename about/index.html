Field Kits Field Notes About Ask Mark

Hi, I'm Mark.

I'm retired, but I still love ugly sales problems. Probably because I spent a lot of years helping customers buy complicated technology, six of them at AWS leading federal partner sales teams. Before AWS it was F5, Red Hat, and a couple of storage companies you've probably forgotten, and before any of that, 20 years as a Marine.

I've been the seller trying to close the deal, the partner trying to make something happen, the manager asking the question nobody wanted to answer, and the guy one level up wondering why the forecast moved again.

These days I mostly help people figure out what's stuck: a deal that isn't moving, a rep you're not sure about, a territory that doesn't add up, a partner that stays busy and never produces anything, or a number that looked fine in January and ridiculous by April. Some of that is just math. A lot of it is asking the right question before everybody loses another month.

A few things I see a lot

The deal everybody thinks should close but really needs to be killed. Good meetings. Nice emails. Lots of interest. Nobody on their side has committed to anything.

The pipeline that looks fine until you ask what's real. $8 million turns into $3.2 million fast once you ask who has money and who can sign.

The rep who might have a problem, or might have a bad territory. Those are two different problems, and people get hurt when managers mix them up.

The partner with plenty of activity and nothing to show for it. Lots of meetings, a joint deck, no deals.

The quota that came from somebody who's never seen your territory. Sometimes the math works and sometimes it doesn't. Better to know which.

The comp plan that gets a little worse every year while the quota goes up. Nobody announces it. Everybody notices. If the number's crazy, here's how I'd push back.

The plan that assumes AI will make every rep 30% more productive. It can write the email. It can't get you in front of somebody who can sign.

Ugly deals especially. Stuck, overcomplicated, single-threaded, a flaky customer, and somehow still alive after six months of everybody saying it's close. I like helping somebody talk it through and figure out what's actually going on.

Who I've helped

Over the years I've helped sellers and managers from Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Oracle, and plenty of smaller companies. Sometimes it's a deal or a territory, sometimes a rep. A lot of the time they just want somebody outside the org chart to look at it with them.
