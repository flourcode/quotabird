About Mark

Who's behind the tools, the situations I see most, and how to reach me.

Hi, I'm Mark.

I've spent a lot of years helping customers buy complicated technology. For six of them I led federal partner sales teams at AWS. Before that F5, Red Hat, and a couple of storage companies you've probably forgotten. Before all of that, 20 years as a Marine.

Along the way I've been the seller defending the deal, the partner trying to help, the manager asking annoying questions, and the guy one level up explaining why the forecast moved again.

QuotaBird is where I put the things I've found useful. Most of the tools are free. Most take a few minutes. None of them require you to adopt a new sales religion. Use whatever helps. If you want to know how I think, here's stuff I like.

A few things I see a lot

Usually you've got more than one problem.

The forecast call has become the job. Your manager wants a status on everything by noon, the CRM wants a next step on every line, and the customer is the last person you talk to each day.

The pipeline looks better than it really is. Plenty of opportunities in the CRM, but once you ask about budget, timing and who actually wants the thing, the number gets smaller fast.

The partner likes you, but nothing is happening. Plenty of meetings, maybe a joint deck. Nobody can point to the accounts where the two companies are actually trying to win something together.

The team is busy, but there isn't much rhythm. The forecast call happens. The one-on-ones happen. Nobody is sure what the team should do differently this week to make next quarter better.

The number from above doesn't match what you see below. Leadership has a growth number. Your team has a pipeline. Somehow those two numbers are supposed to meet.

A team can be behind plan for a lot of reasons. One seller needs help getting in front of customers. Another is doing fine and wants you to stay out of the way. Someone else thinks the year is already gone. Sometimes the rep is fine and the territory, the comp plan or the pipeline they inherited isn't. Figure out which problem you have before you start fixing things. That's what the tools here are for, and it's most of what I do.

Who I've helped

Over the years I've helped sellers and managers from Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Akamai, Databricks, Oracle, government, and plenty of smaller companies. Sometimes it's a deal. Sometimes a territory. Sometimes a rep. Sometimes they just need somebody outside the chain of command to tell them whether they're crazy.

Stuff I like

Some sales. Some just because I like them.

Books

The Qualified Sales Leader

John McMahon

Probably the one I'd hand to someone managing serious enterprise sellers.

Fanatical Prospecting

Jeb Blount

If prospecting is the part of the job you keep finding reasons not to do.

Getting to Yes

Roger Fisher, William Ury & Bruce Patton

Negotiating without turning every conversation into a hostage situation.

The Little Red Book of Selling

Jeffrey Gitomer

Old-school, occasionally corny, and still right about a surprising amount.

How to Say It

Rosalie Maggio

Not really a sales book. Useful when you know what you mean but can't find the words.

Atomic Habits

James Clear

Useful mostly because sales careers are built out of a lot of boring things done repeatedly.

Meditations

Marcus Aurelius

Two thousand years old and still useful when the forecast call starts getting stupid.

Tao Te Ching

Lao Tzu

A useful reminder that forcing things usually makes them worse. Applies to selling, managing, and most meetings.

The Bezos Blueprint

Carmine Gallo

A good look at why Amazon communicates the strange way it does.

Amazon Unbound

Brad Stone

Less about selling than understanding how one very large company thinks.

Podcasts

The Brutal Truth About Sales & Selling

Brian Burns

Actual selling. Not much incense.

Hidden Brain

Shankar Vedantam

People are weird. Helpful to remember when selling to them or managing them.

Freakonomics Radio

Stephen J. Dubner

Incentives explain a lot of behavior, including some very stupid sales behavior.

Marketplace

NPR

Twenty-some minutes and you have a decent idea what's happening in the economy.

Pivot

Kara Swisher & Scott Galloway

Tech, business, politics, and two people disagreeing with each other.

How to Be a Better Human

TED

Pretty much what it says.

The Daily Stoic

Ryan Holiday

Useful before certain forecast calls.

The Side Hustle Show

Nick Loper

For people who occasionally wonder what else they could build.

Radiolab

WNYC

Good stories about things I didn't know I was interested in.

Stuff You Should Know

Josh Clark & Chuck Bryant

Has nothing to do with quota. That's partly why I like it.
