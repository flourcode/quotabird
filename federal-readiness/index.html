What a federal business needs

A federal customer liking your product isn't the same as a federal business. A business needs all five of these. Miss one and the deal waits, usually until next fiscal year. I'll ask about each one the way a federal buyer would, not the way the pitch deck does.

CUSTOMER: Is there an actual federal customer asking for this?

Someone, by name and office, who has asked for the thing. Interest after a demo doesn't count yet. Next: Write down what they asked for, in their words.

MONEY: Do you know where the funding would come from?

Federal money has a name: an appropriation, a program line, a color of money with a fiscal year attached. "They have budget" isn't an answer. Next: Find out which budget it would come from, and when that money is available.

PATH: Do you know how they can legally buy it?

A contract vehicle they already use, a marketplace, a partner's contract, or something else. If nobody can name it, there's no way to buy. Next: Write down how they'd buy it, and who runs that process.

PARTNERS: Do you know whether you need a prime, reseller, marketplace or other partner?

Most commercial companies sell their first federal deals through somebody who already holds the paper. Next: Decide which kind you need, and talk to one this month.

TEAM: Do you have somebody who understands how to navigate all of the above?

Federal is a different motion. Someone who has done it saves you a fiscal year. Next: Get somebody involved who has sold to the government before, even part time.

Questions

Who's answering?
The AI version of Mark: set up to answer the way he would after twenty-plus years in federal sales. Start with the mission, follow the money and the path through primes and partners, arm your champion, deal with the blocker, do something in the next 72 hours. It's not the real Mark, and it says so. He's on the Ask Mark page, twenty minutes at a time.
Is this legal or contracting advice?
No. It asks what a federal buyer will ask. For contracts, compliance and authorization, talk to people who do that work for a living.
What happened to the five questions?
They're on this page, under what a federal business needs: customer, money, path, partners and team. The page used to score them. Now you tell Mark where you are and he tests it against all five.
Can I trust it?
Trust it like advice from an experienced coach who wasn't in the room. It knows how federal deals actually move, and it'll tell you what to do this week. It doesn't know your customer's org chart or this year's budget line, so check any name, vehicle or number before you repeat it to a customer.
Does anything I type leave my device?
Yes. This page uses AI, unlike the tools. When you press the button, what you typed goes to Google's Gemini through QuotaBird's own server, and the answer comes back here. The server doesn't save it, and QuotaBird never sees it. Don't paste anything that isn't yours to share: nothing classified or controlled unclassified, nothing marked internal, nothing about a customer you wouldn't put in an email to a stranger.
