What a real partner actually does

Everybody gets along. There have been plenty of meetings, maybe a joint slide deck. The question is whether anyone can point to the account where the two companies are actually trying to win something together. I test every partner against these five.

SOURCED: Have they brought you an opportunity you didn't find yourself?

If they've never handed you a deal you didn't already have, you're working for them. One sourced deal is worth more than a year of joint webinars. Test it: Ask them for one opportunity this month. Their answer is the diagnosis.

ACCOUNTS: Is there a named account both sides are working right now?

Not a target list. A customer, a requirement, and two sellers who know each other's names. Test it: Pick three accounts, get both sellers on one call, and agree who does what by when.

OWNER: Does someone on their side carry a number that includes you?

Partnerships mostly run on comp plans. If nobody at the partner gets paid when you win, every deal is a favor, and people run out of favors. Test it: Find out whose comp plan includes you. If it's nobody, that's the problem.

PLAN: Is there a co-sell plan with dates on it, not a deck?

A plan is three accounts, two dates and an owner for each. If it takes a deck to explain, it isn't a plan. Test it: Replace the deck with one page.

PULL: Would they call you if you stopped calling them?

A real partner notices when you go quiet. Most won't. Test it: Stop calling for two weeks and see what happens.

Questions

Who's answering?
The AI version of Mark: set up to answer the way he would after twenty-plus years in federal sales. Start with the mission, follow the money and the path through primes and partners, arm your champion, deal with the blocker, do something in the next 72 hours. It's not the real Mark, and it says so. He's on the Ask Mark page, twenty minutes at a time.
Does it work for a distributor, an SI or a prime?
Yes. The direction the paper flows doesn't matter. What matters is whether anyone on the other side is accountable for a deal with your name on it.
What happened to the five questions?
They're on this page, under what a real partner actually does: sourced, accounts, owner, plan and pull. The page used to score them. Now you tell Mark what's going on, and he tests it against all five and asks the follow-up a questionnaire can't.
Can I trust it?
Trust it like advice from an experienced coach who wasn't in the room. It knows how federal deals actually move, and it'll tell you what to do this week. It doesn't know your customer's org chart or this year's budget line, so check any name, vehicle or number before you repeat it to a customer.
Does anything I type leave my device?
Yes. This page uses AI, unlike the tools. When you press the button, what you typed goes to Google's Gemini through QuotaBird's own server, and the answer comes back here. The server doesn't save it, and QuotaBird never sees it. Don't paste anything that isn't yours to share: nothing classified or controlled unclassified, nothing marked internal, nothing about a customer you wouldn't put in an email to a stranger.
