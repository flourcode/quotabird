How the comparison works

An offer letter shows OTE, which is what you make at exactly 100% of quota. Nobody should change jobs on that number. This compares what the offer pays at the attainment you can realistically expect there with what your job pays now at the attainment you actually hit.

Use two different attainments on purpose. You know your track record where you are. At a new company you're guessing, the territory is new, and the quota may be harder; Bridge Group's 2026 study found 48% of AEs hit quota. If you're not sure, use something lower for the offer.

A normal year is base plus variable at that attainment. Year one also counts the ramp: during the ramp months you earn your guarantee if there is one, and otherwise about half your normal attainment, since new reps rarely sell at full speed. That half is an assumption, and it's shown so you can argue with it. Year one adds any sign-on bonus and subtracts what you'd leave behind: commission on deals that haven't paid yet, a bonus you'd forfeit, stock vesting in the next twelve months. The catch-up month is when the offer's running total passes what you'd have made by staying.

No job to compare it to? Choose Just check the offer. You'll see what it really pays, what year one looks like, how much of the pay rides on quota, and whether the ramp is protected. Whether the quota itself is sane is a separate question: Quota Check answers it.

This covers cash only, at a straight-line rate. It doesn't include accelerators (Pay Check does), or the value of stock, RSUs and ESPPs, which QuotaBird doesn't estimate. It isn't financial advice.

Questions

Does anything I enter leave my device?
No. The math runs right here in your browser. There's no account, and nothing goes to a server or your CRM. QuotaBird counts page views with Google Analytics, but it never sees your numbers, and nothing leaves the page unless you share a result.
Why two different attainments?
Because you know one and you're guessing the other. Use what you actually hit where you are. For the offer, ask what share of the team hit quota last year and how the territory was built; Bridge Group's 2026 study found 48% of AEs hit quota, so 100% is optimistic for most people.
What counts as what I'd leave behind?
Money you'd have been paid by staying and won't be if you leave: commission on closed deals that pays out after your last day, an annual bonus you'd forfeit, stock that vests in the next twelve months. Ask the new company to buy some of it out with a sign-on bonus.
What if the guarantee is a draw I have to pay back?
Then leave the guarantee blank. A recoverable draw is an advance against future commission, not extra money.
How do I know if an offer is good on its own?
Three things decide it: what it pays at a realistic attainment, how much of the pay rides on the quota, and whether the quota is achievable. This page shows the first two; Quota Check shows the third.
